How experience is reused across projects
  1. In project A, the responsible person selects a useful rule, authorizes its transfer and records its source, revision and limitations.
  2. A participant with authorized access creates a copy in project B. The lessons learned library consists of curated company material in the CDE with links to DG. It is neither a sixth graph nor a company-wide set of automatically active rules.
  3. In project B, the team checks compatibility with shared classes, adapts parameters and applicability, and validates the rule against that project’s model.
  4. The responsible person in project B approves the new revision. The projects then evolve independently while retaining the source reference.

Example: “Door” is a company-wide object class. A door-width requirement from project A is copied into B as a proposal; its value and applicability are agreed for B’s conditions.

What “target process” means

This diagram describes the intended business process, not the readiness of the current implementation. LLM policy, CDE integrations, knowledge reuse and the scope of permitted Revit operations require design and verification. Company-wide classes do not imply that the Ontograph must be physically stored inside the CDE product.

Validation issues send the option back for rework. Missing data is supplied and checks are repeated. New requirements go through approval, while previous revisions and decisions remain in the history.
